# CS-255-System-Analysis-and-Design
## DriverPass Project Reflection

### Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to provide better training for students preparing for their driving tests. They wanted a web-based system that would allow customers to take online classes and practice tests, schedule driving lessons, and track their progress. The system also needed to support employees with scheduling, customer information, reports, and different levels of access.

### What did you do particularly well?

I think I did particularly well identifying the different requirements from the DriverPass interview and turning them into a system design. I was able to separate functional and nonfunctional requirements and show how different users would interact with the system. Creating the UML diagrams also helped me better understand how the different parts of the system work together.

### If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve some of my UML diagrams. I would make the diagrams more consistent and add more detail to some of the decision points and interactions between users and the system. After working through the entire project, I have a better understanding of how the diagrams connect to each other and how they can communicate the design more clearly.

### How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I interpreted the user's needs by reviewing the DriverPass interview and identifying what customers and employees needed the system to do. I then used those requirements when creating the use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams. Considering the user's needs is important because a system can work technically but still fail if it does not solve the problems the customer actually has. Keeping the user's needs in mind helps make sure the final system is useful and meets the client's goals.

### How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

I would start by understanding the client's needs and identifying the main users, requirements, and limitations of the system. I would then break the system into smaller processes and use UML diagrams to show the workflows, interactions, and relationships between different parts of the system. In future projects, I would continue using requirements gathering, process modeling, object modeling, and feedback from users throughout the design process instead of waiting until the end to make changes.
